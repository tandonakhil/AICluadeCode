0 · The thesis this whole design is built on

Read this before the screens. Every layout decision below is downstream of it.
The brief's anti-recommendation is binding. INDUSTRY_KB §15.4: reviewers given clearer AI explanations defer more, not less. So the 11pm approval problem cannot be solved by presenting the agent's reasoning beautifully — doing so makes the control weaker. DOMAIN_KB §7.2 is blunter still: the constraint is attention budget, which is fixed, small and already spent by the fortieth item.

So this design does four things instead, and one of them is an inversion.
1. Reduce volume, and show the reduction. N detected → M routed is the largest thing on the Exceptions screen, not a footnote.
2. Make effort scale with risk. Uniform approval undifferentiated by risk is the same as no scrutiny, so the interaction cost of the riskiest item is deliberately higher than the safest.
3. Give the riskiest element the page, not the clearest narrative the page.
4. Demote the explanation. The agent's narrative is the collapsed element on the Review screen; the risk band is the uncollapsible one. This is the inversion of the intuitive design and it is deliberate.
Why collapsing the narrative is not merely symbolic

AC-F12-03 requires the capture record to name which evidence elements the reviewer expanded and which they left collapsed. If the agent's narrative is expanded by default, that label is a constant for every reviewer on every item and carries zero information — F12's ground-truth factory produces a column of identical values. Collapsing it by default turns "did this person open the reasoning?" into a real, per-item, per-reviewer signal that AC-F12-08 can surface to the controller. The design decision and the measurement decision are the same decision.

And the one thing I will not design

FUNCTIONAL_SPEC §12 carries a standing exclusion: no criterion asserts explanation quality and none may be added. I have not read that as a gap and I have not designed toward it. There is no "explanation quality" component in this mockup, no confidence score rendered as a reassuring bar, and no summary-of-the-reasoning hero. Where reasoning appears it appears subordinate to the evidence and collapsed by default.

1 · Visual language — an evidence instrument, not an analytics product

Audience: staff accountant (does), controller (supervises, signs), FP&A (explains). Downstream reader: an external auditor, on paper.
Reference points are a working paper, a bank statement and a legal exhibit — not a SaaS analytics dashboard. This artefact ends up inside an audit workpaper as a PDF, so it has to survive greyscale printing and it must not read as marketing. Density is high on purpose: this is a desktop-web-only MVP1 (PLAN §9.2 A2), designed at 1440×900, and finance users read dense tables fluently.
The single most opinionated decision in the palette

There is no green anywhere in this product.

Green means "fine, move on". That is precisely the affect a depleted reviewer must not be handed, and PLAN §2b already warns that a control which cannot fail trains its reviewer that the dashboard cannot fail. A clean result is therefore rendered in neutral ink, stated as a sentence carrying its coverage, and given no colour at all. Colour in this product is reserved for exactly three things: risk (a three-step single-hue ramp), permanence of refusal (slate), and interactivity (one ink blue). Nothing else is coloured. Non-certified state is carried by a hatch pattern plus a label, never by hue alone — it must survive greyscale and colour-vision deficiency.

Risk is a three-step ordinal ramp in one hue family, not a rainbow, so "worse" is legible without a legend and survives print. Desaturated deliberately — saturated red at close volume becomes wallpaper by item ten.
Typography & numerals

System sans for prose; tabular lining numerals for every figure, without exception — proportional digits in a finance product are a defect, not a preference, because column comparison is the primary reading act. Monospace for amounts, account codes, periods, hashes, decision IDs and dataset versions, so that a bundle hash and a dollar figure are never confusable with body text. Amounts are right-aligned; account codes left. Dark mode is a first-class theme, not a courtesy: this product is used at 11pm on day 3.

2 · Information architecture

Nine screens named by FUNCTIONAL_SPEC §23. I keep every name — routes are already in PLAN §6.2 — and group them three ways.
Grouping argument. The nine screens serve three different jobs and two different people. Work is the staff accountant's night. Govern is the controller's week. Evidence is what leaves the building. Putting Refusals under Evidence rather than in a settings drawer is deliberate: it is an artefact a controller takes to an audit committee, and burying it makes the refusal look like an omission — the exact failure AC-REFUSAL-02 exists to prevent.

Counts in the nav are the volume control, visible before you click

The badge on Review is the number of items routed to this person tonight — not the number the system detected. 3 · Ask — natural-language skill invocation over a declared population

F39 · F38 · AC-F39-09 AC-F39-01 AC-F38-14 AC-F38-10
Dataset selection is a control surface, not a filter widget (INDUSTRY_KB §14), and under-selection is the failure that bites because it is invisible. So I inverted the selector: the user does not pick tables from a list. The skill declares the population it needs, and the screen shows which parts of that declaration are covered and which are not, by name. Under-selection stops being an absence the user cannot perceive and becomes a list of named unmet segments they have to look at. A dropdown cannot do that; a declared-population panel can.

Second consequence: the coverage read-out is not a progress bar. A bar that fills toward the right reads as "70% good". This is a population strip with the gaps drawn in and labelled — the missing segments are the graphic, not the absence of graphic.

4 · Ask — a declared refusal, inline

AC-REFUSAL-03 AC-REFUSAL-05 AC-REFUSAL-06
"Not built yet" and "will never be built" are the same blank screen to a user and opposite answers to an auditor. So they get two structurally different card shapes, two different header words, and two different border treatments — solid slate with a heavy left bar for refused permanently, dashed amber outline for deferred. They are never mixed in one list, and the distinction is carried in the text as well as the form, so it survives greyscale and a screen reader.

5 · Exceptions — volume reduction is the headline, not a metric

AC-F41-09 AC-F29-12 AC-F42-08 AC-F26-10 AC-F28-10 AC-F33-12 AC-F38-15
N detected → M routed to a human is the largest object on this screen. That is a direct reading of AC-F41-09, but I have gone further than "both are visible": the ratio is the page's masthead, because it is the only thing on the screen that tells a reviewer whether the system is working for them or dumping on them. A product whose N/M ratio degrades is failing at its actual job even when every individual finding is correct.

Rows are ordered by risk and banded by risk, and the band is a structural left bar — not a dot, not a coloured word. At close volume a legend-dependent signal is not read. Three steps only.

6 · Coverage states — 100%, 70% and 0% must not be readable as the same thing

AC-F38-07 AC-F38-04 AC-F38-08 AC-F41-10
AC-F38-07 is the criterion I designed hardest against. A 70% clean run and a 100% clean run must be textually different. My answer is that coverage is not a status line placed near the conclusion — coverage changes the grammar of the conclusion itself. At full coverage the sentence has a universal quantifier. Below full coverage the sentence has a bounded one and names the bound. The component that renders a conclusion is structurally incapable of emitting the words "clean" or "no exceptions" unqualified when coverage < 100%, because that string is not reachable in that state.

7 · Review — the 11pm screen. The riskiest element gets the page.

AC-F41-01 AC-F41-02 AC-F41-03 AC-F41-05 AC-F41-06 AC-F41-13 retired 2026-08-03 → AC-F41-22/-23/-24 AC-F36-18 AC-F35-09 AC-F35-05 AC-F32-01
Reading order, top to bottom: risk → evidence → resolution → narrative. The agent's reasoning is last and collapsed. The riskiest ranked element is first, uncollapsed, at the largest type size on the screen. There is no "Approve" button on this screen for a finding — the terminal action is record a resolution, and approval exists only where a posting artefact exists (see §9). That is a direct answer to DOMAIN_KB §10: if the UI makes posting the default terminal state, the safe answer becomes harder to record than the risky one.

All six resolution types are one row of six equal buttons, same size, same weight, none pre-selected. R2 (data-side fix) and R5 (handoff) are the majority outcome and they sit in the same visual rank as R3/R4. Counting interactions to completion: R2 = 3, R5 = 4, R3 = 5, R4 = 5. The safe outcomes are never more effortful than the posting ones — that is AC-F35-05 satisfied by construction rather than by inspection.

Nothing on this screen is reachable only by hover or by a live query. That is a real design constraint imposed by AC-F41-04: the rendered view retained as evidence has to reproduce what the approver saw, so a tooltip-only fact is a fact that vanishes from the audit record.

A toast that says "this run is stale" is dismissed by a tired reviewer in under a second and the approve button is still there. So the action area is structurally replaced, not decorated. The only forward action is to open the superseding run.

11 · Known-error probes — invisible before, revealed immediately after

AC-F41-08 AC-F12-05 · one design call beyond the criteria, argued
The criteria require only that a probe be indistinguishable from a genuine proposal by anything rendered before disposition. They are silent on afterwards. My call: reveal it the instant the disposition is submitted, with the correct answer.

Reasoning: the stated purpose of injected probes in INDUSTRY_KB §15.4 is to keep attention live. A probe that is never revealed is a measurement instrument only — it measures decay without arresting it. A probe revealed at the moment of disposition is a measurement and a calibration signal delivered at the one moment the reviewer is still holding the item in their head. It is also the fairest option for the staff accountant, who is otherwise being silently scored.

The cost I accept: reviewers will learn the probe rate and may recalibrate against it. That is a real weakness. I judge it smaller than the alternative, and it is mitigated by varying the rate. The consequence for the build: no component may render probe status anywhere before disposition — not a badge, not a class name in the DOM, not a differing skeleton height. That is a testable statement and I would like the UX suite to assert it.

12 · Dispositions — the prediction, and the product marking its own homework

AC-F32-09 AC-F32-01 AC-F35-07 AC-F35-06
The forward-disposition field is the most retrofit-hostile requirement in the backlog and the one most likely to feel like a tax. Three design moves stop it feeling like one.
1. It is not a field, it is the sentence you are already writing. "This clears by ▾". The save control reads "Record — clears by 2026-07", so the prediction is the shape of the action rather than a validation error that appears after you thought you were done. Until it is set the button reads "Record — clears by …" and does nothing.
2. It is never pre-filled (a defaulted prediction is a manufactured one, and would poison the hit rate that makes this control worth having), but the three most common answers are one tap.
3. It is framed as a promise the product makes to you, not a hoop: "We will check in 2026-07 and tell you if it did not." Micro-copy converts an obligation into a service.

One risk I want on the record: showing the user their own hit rate next to the field may push people toward conservative, easily-met predictions, which would inflate the measure. I still recommend showing it — an invisible score is worse — but the controller's view should watch for horizon drift, and I would like that watched in the first production period rather than assumed away.

14 · Monitors — the controller's screen, and the product marking itself

AC-F32-10 AC-F41-07 AC-F12-08 AC-F36-19 AC-F9-08
The forward-disposition hit rate is the masthead of this screen, not a tile. It is the product's own falsifiability measure and it is displayed whether it is good or bad. Everything else on this screen measures the agents and the reviewers; this one measures us, and putting it anywhere but first would be a tell.

No metric on this screen is coloured good or bad. A high override rate could mean a reviewer is catching things or that an agent is misfiring; a low one could mean the agent is right or that nobody is reading. Colouring it would assert an interpretation the data does not support, and a green dashboard is exactly the artefact that teaches a controller their controls cannot fail. Figures are ink; only escalations take risk colour.

17 · What I am asking the human to decide

Four items. Everything else in this document is a call I have already made.
1 · AC-F41-03 — I am not asking for it to be relaxed. I am asking for it to be strengthened.

FUNCTIONAL_SPEC §25.3 offers to renegotiate the riskiest-element criterion if I judge it to trespass on design. It does not trespass. Reading order and non-collapsibility are exactly the right level of specification — behavioural, testable, and silent on pixels. But they are insufficient: an element can be first in the DOM and uncollapsed and still be visually recessive, which would satisfy the criterion and defeat its purpose. I would like one more behavioural clause, issued as a new ID by functional-design-agent rather than assumed by me: the riskiest element renders at the largest type size present on the Review screen, and no other element on that screen renders at an equal or larger size. That is checkable by comparing computed font sizes and still fixes no number.

2 · A design intent with no acceptance criterion behind it — a routing budget

Volume reduction is the mechanism the research supports, and AC-F41-09 makes the volume visible — but nothing in the 186 criteria makes it bounded. I would like a configured per-reviewer per-night routing budget: when a run would exceed it, the run says so and a controller must either raise the cap (recorded as a control event) or split the queue. That turns volume reduction from an aspiration into a structural limit. I am proposing it, not adding it — new scope is plan-agent's lane and it would need a criterion from functional-design-agent. If it is not taken, the 11pm problem is mitigated by this design but not bounded by it, and that should be a recorded choice rather than an oversight.

3 · Probe reveal timing

I have designed immediate post-disposition reveal (§11). The criteria permit it and are silent on it. It is a judgement call with a real cost — reviewers learn the probe rate — and responsible-ai-architect should have a view before it is built.

4 · A recorded requirement this pass does not cover

PROJECT_CONTEXT.md's header still reads "Surfaces: desktop web · mobile web · native mobile (multi-surface)", while the Decisions Log and PLAN §9.2 A2 scope MVP1 to one surface, desktop web, with F23/F24 deferred and F24 recommended for rejection on control grounds. I have designed desktop web only. There is no mobile layout in this document and no responsive breakpoint below tablet in the product design. I am flagging the header rather than silently satisfying the narrower reading — if multi-surface is still binding, this gate needs a second pass and F24 needs re-deciding, because approving from mobile is the lowest-scrutiny approval surface that exists and 11pm is exactly when it would be used.
