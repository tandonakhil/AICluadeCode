1 · The Council Mark

Six teal threads converging on a neutral core, with a single gold pull-line above — the six SDLC disciplines, the Conclave core, and you. The marketing site defines three tiers and one hard colour law, quoted verbatim from site.css: “GOLD IS USED ONLY FOR THE PULL-LINE + ITS TERMINUS DOT — never the core, never the threads.”

A rule the first visual pass broke

My earlier sidebar used a gold filled disc as an app icon. That violates the mark’s own colour law — gold on a core. It is replaced below by the real geometry, with the core in --neutral-core where it belongs.
Tier 3 — carried verbatim

The pull-line signature

34px. Marketing uses it on /how and /what at human-decision beats. That is precisely what this product marks, so it is reused for its own meaning, unmodified.

Approved

Gold. A human approved. In this product: the Approve control on the approval screen, and a recorded approval.

Sent back

Rust. A human rejected or returned it. This variant already exists in how.html — I did not create it.

Not concluded

Slate, dashed. NEW. The one state marketing has no beat for: the system abstained, so no human decision was ever offered. Dashed because it is an absence of a decision, not a decision.

The stat strip is the single most directly reusable marketing component. It was already the right shape for “6 routed / 1 abstention / 70% coverage”. Two adaptations: the figures are mono, not serif, because they are ledger quantities that must align in a column and serif numerals do not; and a figure may take a severity colour, so 70% reads as the partial-coverage warning it is rather than as an achievement in teal.

4 · Where the serif goes, and why the product is narrower

Marketing

Georgia carries almost everything with a voice: h1.q / h2.q (the site is built as questions), .home-center h1, .who-panel h2, .ex h3, .spine h2, .pullquote, .stat .num, and the whole of .artifact.

Product — deliberately narrower

Serif is used for: the product’s own voice (refusals, abstentions), the artifact kind label and any quoted statement, and the page-opening headline on the two narrative surfaces (Close, Period record). Not on data-object titles, table headers, or figures.

This is a deliberate choice, not an oversight — and it follows the same rule

The marketing rule is not “headings are serif”. It is the serif carries Conclave’s voice; the sans carries the evidence. On a marketing site nearly everything is voice, so the serif is nearly everywhere. In this product nearly everything is evidence — account codes, amounts, coverage, versions — so the same rule produces a much smaller serif footprint. Same principle, different content mix.

Two places the earlier pass got it wrong and this corrects: (a) marketing's .artifact is serif on cream and my evidence panel was sans on cream — now the kind label and quoted statement are serif, while the table inside stays sans/mono, because serif tabular figures at 15px in a dense grid measurably hurt scanning; (b) the Close overview headline is now serif, because “Day 3 of 8. Five things need you” is the product speaking, not a data label.

What cannot come across at all

The site's question-and-answer structure. Marketing is built as a chain of questions (h1.q, .answer, the chain-nav that walks Why → What → How → Who). It is a rhetorical spine, and it is the best thing about that site. A close queue has no rhetorical spine — it has a task order. The tone transfers (plain, unhedged, willing to say what it will not do); the structure cannot.

The gold underline at scale. On marketing it marks one phrase per page. In a product with forty findings a night it would become decoration within a week, so it is capped at one per page-opening and is not available inside lists.
