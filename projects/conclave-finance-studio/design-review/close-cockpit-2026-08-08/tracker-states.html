The close tracker · every state it can be in

Four states, and none of them is a progress bar.

The tracker is drawn from the declared close calendar (close.pilot_2026_06@1: five checkpoints at 08:00 UTC on close days 1–5). A passed checkpoint is filled neutral ink and never green — a cut-off that has gone by is a fact, not an achievement. What is drawn in colour is the gap between the checkpoint the warehouse refresh was due by and the one it actually landed in; the staleness sentence is that gap’s label, not a second assertion beside it.

No close calendar is declared for this ledger, so where this close stands cannot be stated and neither can the staleness of its data.
The data as-of — 03 Jul 2026 06:15 UTC — is an absolute timestamp and is not a close-relative figure. It is shown under its own name and never under a close-relative one. (AC-F38-11 unmet; deferred-substitution register 6.) Declaring a calendar for this ledger is what makes the tracker exist; until then this block is what renders, on every screen, with no control that dismisses it.

This is the state that decides whether the tracker is honest. A tracker that quietly drew five grey stops and no marker would look like a close that has not started, which is a claim nobody made. An unknown ledger and a ledger whose declared calendar reference is wrong are the same state to everything downstream — no close clock — and both take this path.
