What you are looking at

The extrapolation trap, refused. The analyst asks for something that would be extremely easy to answer wrongly — the corpus holds fully-projected-future-test-year cases and vertically integrated cases, just never both in the same case. A blend of the two is fluent, well-sourced-looking, and is the catastrophic harm this product exists to avoid.

  • The refusal is not styled as an error. No red, no warning triangle, no alert role, and the words "error", "failed" and "problem" appear nowhere in it (AC-F38-02). It sits in the same panel treatment as an answer, distinguished by a dashed left rule and a neutral No answer given label. Refusal is the system working.
  • It names the gap. Missing dimension, the question's value, and what the corpus actually holds instead — as a structured block, not buried in a sentence (AC-F31-05, AC-F38-03).
  • The 40 examined cases are visible on the refusal itself (AC-F38-04), and the full coverage panel is rendered alongside it (AC-F37-07). This is the difference between "we found nothing" and "we looked at nothing" — compare screen 07, where the same panel looks completely different.
  • No citation card is rendered (AC-F36-09) and no docket appears except in the list of what was examined (AC-F31-11). A refusal that shows citations manufactures the appearance of support.
  • Next steps are offered without suggesting a workaround. They are ways to ask a question the corpus can answer, never ways to get the number anyway.
