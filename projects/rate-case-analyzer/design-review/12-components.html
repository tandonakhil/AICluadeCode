What you are looking at

The system sheet. Everything on screens 01–11 is assembled from what is on this page. If a future feature needs a visual treatment that is not here, that is a design decision to be made, not improvised.

The section that matters most is the 14×6 encoding. There are fourteen document types and six claim statuses, and a naïve design would need eighty-four distinct treatments — which is not a design, it is a legend nobody reads. The resolution is to collapse each axis into a small number of visually distinct weights, and to carry the full precision as text on every instance.

The 14 × 6 problem, and how it is solved

Axis 1 · document_type — 14 ranked values → 4 authority tiers, shown as a countable spine

The fourteen values are already ranked 1–14 by the domain KB. Not all fourteen need equal visual weight: what an analyst is actually scanning for is "is this an outcome, a recommendation, an ask, or argument?" — a four-way distinction. So the ranking is bucketed into four tiers rendered as a vertical spine of four segments with N filled. It is countable, it survives greyscale, it survives being read aloud ("authority tier 4 of 4"), and it needs no colour. The exact enum value and its rank are printed as text on every card, so nothing is lost (AC-F36-03).

  • Tier 4 of 4 — Outcome documents. What was actually granted or implemented.
    1 FINAL_ORDER · 2 ORDER_ON_REHEARING · 3 APPROVED_SETTLEMENT · 4 COMPLIANCE_FILING
    The only tier from which an AUTHORIZED claim may be written. Enforced at write time, not query time.
  • Tier 3 of 4 — Recommendations and official positions. Someone with standing proposed this. Never an outcome.
    5 PROPOSED_ORDER_ALJ · 6 STAFF_REPORT_OR_TESTIMONY · 7 INTERVENOR_TESTIMONY
  • Tier 2 of 4 — Party filings and the ask. What the utility requested, and the schedules behind it.
    8 UTILITY_REBUTTAL_TESTIMONY · 9 UTILITY_DIRECT_TESTIMONY · 10 APPLICATION · 11 EXHIBIT_SCHEDULE
    An EXHIBIT_SCHEDULE inherits its parent filing's identity at ingestion; one with no resolvable parent never enters the corpus.
  • Tier 1 of 4 — Record and argument. Useful context; never a fact source for an outcome.
    12 DATA_REQUEST_RESPONSE · 13 HEARING_TRANSCRIPT · 14 BRIEF
    Deferred from MVP1 ingestion, but the tier exists now so adding them later is a pipeline change, not a design change.

Axis 2 · claim_status — 6 values → 3 chip families

The distinction that carries the harm is not six-way, it is three-way: was this granted, was it merely proposed, or is it absent? Confusing the first two is the project's named worst harm. So the six values collapse into three chip families with sharply different construction — filled, hatched-outline, dashed-outline — and every chip prints its family, its exact enum value, and a plain-English gloss. Colour is the last of four redundant channels, not the first.

Family 1 · OUTCOME — solid fill, highest contrast

OutcomeAUTHORIZED— granted by commission order OutcomeSETTLED— agreed in an approved settlement OutcomeIMPLEMENTED— in effect per compliance tariff

Family 2 · POSITION — hatched outline, never filled

PositionREQUESTED— what the utility asked for; not granted PositionRECOMMENDED— proposed by staff or an intervenor; not granted

The hatch is the point. A hatched chip reads as provisional at a glance and never resolves into the solid outcome chip, at any size or contrast.

Family 3 · ABSENT — dashed outline, no fill

AbsentNOT STATED— the document does not state this figure

Never blank, never a dash alone, never omitted. A dashed border is the visual grammar of "there is a slot here and it is deliberately empty" — distinct from both a value and a missing card.

Colour

Warm paper, cool ink, one accent, and functional colour used in exactly three places: the position family (amber), the caveat family (the same amber), and system failure (red — the only red in the product). No gradients. No green: an answer is not a "success" and a refusal is not a "failure", and colouring them that way would teach analysts to route around the guardrail. Light values shown; the dark theme is a token swap under prefers-color-scheme and preserves every contrast ratio.

Typography

Three system stacks, no web fonts — nothing to download, nothing to fail, no layout shift, and no CDN call from a tool handling regulatory material. Tabular numerals are on globally so columns of ROE figures align. Body measure is capped at 68 characters; the dense reference material lives in tables and cards, not in long prose.

Accessibility rules that are not negotiable

  1. Authority and status are never encoded by colour alone. Every instance carries the enum value as text plus a plain-English gloss, and a non-colour visual channel (spine segment count, chip fill construction).
  2. Every citation card shows a date, labelled as an order date or a filing date. There is no card layout in which the date is optional.
  3. The refusal panel carries no alert or error role, no danger semantics, and none of the words "error", "failed" or "problem". The system-error panel carries role="alert" and is the only component that does.
  4. No coverage region ever renders as a bare empty list. Empty regions render explicit text; a zero-candidate coverage renders a structurally different element from a zero-included coverage.
  5. WCAG AA contrast minimum for all text, in both light and dark themes. Non-text boundaries meet 3:1.
  6. Semantic structure: one h1 per screen, real landmarks, real lists and tables with headers, visible 2px focus outlines, and the coverage bar carries a text alternative stating the same counts.
  7. No icon carries meaning on its own. There are almost no icons; the ones present are decorative and aria-hidden.
