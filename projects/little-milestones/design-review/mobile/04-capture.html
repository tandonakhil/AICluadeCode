Capture — the centre [+] action

The one genuinely new thing mobile brings: the camera is already in the parent’s hand. This is the flow from the [+] tab-bar button to a memory on the journey, in three taps and a sentence.

Privacy decisions flagged for security-architect (all in this flow): (1) whether captured images are written to the device camera roll before upload — the design says no, capture into app-scoped temporary storage and delete after successful upload; (2) how long a queued offline photo may sit unencrypted in the app sandbox; (3) whether the image picker uses the OS limited-selection API (iOS PHPicker / Android Photo Picker) so the app never holds full-library permission; (4) EXIF/GPS strip — the design assumes it happens client-side and server-side, never one or the other.

2 · Permission — pre-prompt first, always. The OS dialog is one-shot: a denied prompt is expensive to recover from. So the app explains why in its own card (with the verbatim privacy line) and only then triggers the system prompt. Denied → a calm inline card: “Camera access is off. You can still write moments down, or turn camera access on in Settings” with a deep link to app settings. Never a nag loop, never a modal that reappears.

4 · Optimistic save + offline queue. The moment appears on the journey immediately, marked as pending, and uploads when connectivity returns. This is the single most important offline behaviour in the app: a parent capturing a first step in a basement with no signal must never lose it. If the upload ultimately fails, the card shows a plain “Couldn’t upload — try again” with a retry, and the memory stays on the device until it succeeds or the parent deletes it.
