/what redesign — three live examples, for approval

What can it build?

Three kinds of application — support chatbots, automated workflows, and knowledge assistants — each delivered through the same nine gates, from the same templates Conclave maintains for itself.

These are the shapes Conclave's template catalog actually supports today. Watch each one work, not just read about it.

Support chatbots

Answers with a citation, and an honest "I don't know"

Live example

What a build produces

A curated answer corpus, a citation under every reply, an explicit refusal path, and a one-tap route to a person.

What you approve

The question domain and honesty guardrail at intake; tone at design; retention rules at architecture; the launch itself.

What it is not: an oracle. It won't answer outside its approved knowledge set or improvise policy — a send-back at test is the enforced consequence of trying.

What a build produces

Discrete, inspectable steps; defined checkpoints that pause for a human decision; a full replayable run record.

What you approve

Which steps may run without you at planning; failure behavior at architecture; test evidence the checkpoints actually stop the run.

What it is not: autonomous control. It prepares, drafts, and executes the routine — the calls that matter stop and wait for a person, written into the architecture record.
Knowledge assistants

Searches by meaning, cites what it found

Live example
onboarding-guide.pdf benefits-2026.pdf leave-policy.pdf org-chart.pdf

What a build produces

A retrieval-backed assistant over a corpus you control — "not in the corpus" is a valid, visible result, not an invitation to invent one.

What you approve

What enters the corpus at intake, revisited on every enhancement; the citation format at design; accuracy evidence at test.

What it is not: a general search engine or a chatbot that happens to have read your files. When the corpus is silent, so is it — it points to a person instead of a guess.
How this differs from today's /what: the "what a build produces" and "what you approve" prose is condensed into two short columns per type (still complete, half the reading), and each type now opens with a small looping-on-demand demo instead of only description — a chat exchange playing out with a citation and one honest refusal, a workflow pipeline lighting up step by step and visibly pausing at its human checkpoint, and a document search resolving into a cited answer with a "not in the corpus" case included. Every demo replays on click and is scripted/deterministic (no live model calls, no external requests) — it's illustrative, matching the same "canonical build, not a customer story" framing already used on /how. All three respect prefers-reduced-motion (typing indicators and cursors are skipped; states appear instantly). Reload or click "↻ Replay" on any demo to see it run.
