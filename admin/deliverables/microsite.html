A governed multi-agent SDLC

Ship AI products with a human approving every gate.

Conclave is a 9-gate multi-agent system that plans, designs, builds, and tests AI-centric applications for the energy industry — and stops for your sign-off at every one of them. A roster of specialist agents convened per project, deliberating under one orchestrator, with the human always holding final say.

Who it's for

Governed, not autonomous.

Teams building AI-centric applications for the energy industry — GenAI chatbots, agentic workflows, RAG/knowledge-base apps — who need real engineering discipline without hand-rolling it for every new project.

Two axes, never mixed

/new-project, /enhance-project, /modify-feature, /consult build and evolve individual applications. /admin-panel evolves Conclave itself — never reachable from a project command, never touches projects/.

The value proposition

Every project gets the same disciplined pipeline — Plan, Architecture, Code, Test, Review, Deploy — with the right specialist SMEs convened for its actual risk profile, and a human who never loses the ability to stop, redirect, or override at any gate.

Architecture

Layers of agents, not a single monolithic builder.

The human approves. One orchestrator dispatches. Agents never call each other directly — every hand-off runs through the orchestrator and through shared markdown state on disk.

The add-agent flow

Real precedent: deliverables-agent and synthetic-data-agent

mas-architect gave advisory review of each proposed role — checking for overlap with existing agents, deciding core-vs-optional status and gate placement — purely advisory, never self-approving. The human reviewed and approved each recommendation explicitly before mas-registrar scaffolded the actual agent file. synthetic-data-agent's full path: proposed 2026-07-11 from a real concrete need, advisory-reviewed, human-approved via checkbox roadmap review 2026-07-12, then built — no step skipped.

This microsite itself

Even this page followed the pattern: ui-ux-designer proposed a visual direction (informed by real reference sites), the human reviewed a live preview with 5 selectable color themes, chose Copper + Dark, requested scroll animations, and approved the final build — nothing shipped without that review step.
